The waste hierarchy

Most preferred first.

Product that can be worn is worn. Recycling is for what is left, not a way around the question.

  1. {{hx:repeat}}

    Resale and reuse first

    Wearable product goes back to market through controlled, contracted channels.

  2. {{hx:donate}}

    Donation through vetted networks

    Wearable goods suited to donation reach vetted partners, with receipts kept on file.

  3. {{hx:recycle}}

    Certified recycling last

    What cannot be worn is recycled through certified partners: soles reground, textiles recovered.

Why now

Destroying excess is ending. Proving where it goes is the new standard.

Three forces now apply to unsold footwear and apparel at the same time. Each asks the same question: where did the product go, and can you prove it?

EU

From 19 July 2026

A ban on destroying unsold goods

Large companies may no longer destroy unsold apparel, clothing accessories or footwear placed on the EU market, and must disclose what they discard and why.

California

Phasing in from 2026

Producer responsibility for textiles

SB 707 makes producers of apparel and footwear sold in California responsible for collection, repair, reuse and recycling, through a state-approved program.
