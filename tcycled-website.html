Questions, answered.

Does it cost anything?

No. The bag, the label and the app are free.

How do I get a tcycled bag?

Ask for one when you order from a partner shop, at the checkout or in the app, and it comes in your parcel. No bag yet? Use your own box with a free label from the app.

When do I get my voucher?

As soon as the carrier scans your bag for the first time, or when store staff scan the cap. Usually that's the day you send it.

My clothes are torn or stained. Can I still send them?

Yes, as long as they're clean and dry. Damaged pieces are repaired, remade or recycled into new fibre.

Can I send shoes?

Yes, tied together in pairs. A single shoe can't be worn again, so please keep those out.

Where do my clothes go?

To Artus in Bad Oldesloe, where every bag is weighed and sorted by hand. See what happens next.

What are Loops?

You get 1 Loop for every bag that arrives. Spend three Loops for €5 off a product made with recycled fibre.

Second life

Every piece gets the best life it still has.

Every bag is weighed and sorted by hand in Bad Oldesloe. Each piece goes to the best use it still has, always in the same order, and the app shows you where yours went.

From your hallway to its next owner.

  1. Your hallwayYou fill the bag and scan the cap.
  2. On its wayThe carrier's first scan unlocks your voucher.
  3. WeighedArtus weighs the bag in Bad Oldesloe. Loops and the donation follow.
  4. Sorted by handTrained sorters pick the best next use for every piece.
  5. Second lifeWorn again, remade or spun into new fibre.

In this order. Always.

Reuse comes first, because nothing new has to be made. Recycling comes next. Energy recovery is only for what nothing else can save.

ReuseWorn again as second-hand

Pieces that can be worn again are sold as second-hand clothing.

It's the best outcome, because the water and energy that went into making them keep working.

RefashionRepaired or remade by partner workshops

A broken zip, a missing button or a small tear sends a piece to a partner workshop.

It comes back repaired, or remade into something new, like a bag or a patchwork jacket.

New fibreRecycled into fibre for new clothes

Worn-out pieces of suitable material are shredded and spun into new yarn.

That yarn goes into new clothes, closing the loop. You can spend Loops on products made with it.

Other productsInsulation, cleaning cloths, filling

Fibre that can't become clothing again becomes insulation, cleaning cloths or filling.

It replaces new material in products that last for years.

Energy recoveryOnly for wet or mouldy items

Wet or mouldy items can't be sorted safely, so they're used to make energy.

That's why we ask for clean, dry clothes: it keeps this tier as small as possible.

How we count.

Weighed, not guessed

Every bag is weighed when it arrives. Your impact is calculated from your own kilos, not an average bag.

Sources next to numbers

Water and CO₂e come from published figures per kilo of reused clothing. The app shows the source under every number.

Rounded on purpose

About 98,400 litres, not 98,437.2. Honest numbers don't need decimals.

The rules are coming. You'll have the numbers.

EU textile rules arrive in steps. tcycled records what you'll need for each one, from the first bag on.

Today
  1. 1 Jan 2025Separate collectionEU countries must collect textiles separately from other waste.
  2. Oct 2025Textile EPR becomes lawThe revised Waste Framework Directive enters into force.
  3. 19 Jul 2026No destroying unsold clothesThe ESPR ban applies to large companies.
  4. 2 Mar 2027One reporting formatDiscarded unsold products are reported in a standard EU format.
  5. 17 Apr 2028EPR in every member stateProducers pay for collecting and sorting the textiles they sell.
  6. 19 Jul 2030Medium-sized companies tooThe destruction ban extends to medium-sized companies.
