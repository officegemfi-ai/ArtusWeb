What we do

ResaleGraded stock into reuse, wholesale, recommerce and authorized markets — with the next destination on record.

Regulatory momentum

Producer responsibility is becoming law on both sides of the Atlantic.

Regulation is turning textile take-back from a voluntary program into a documented obligation. Artus runs the operations and produces the evidence.

2008France

The first textile EPR scheme

Producers fund collection, sorting and recycling through the Refashion eco-organization, expanded by the AGEC anti-waste law.

In force · eco-modulated fees
2024California

SB 707, the first US textile EPR law

The Responsible Textile Recovery Act requires producers to join a responsibility organization and fund collection, repair, reuse and recycling.

Program implementation under way
2025European Union

EPR in every member state

The revised Waste Framework Directive makes producer responsibility for textiles mandatory across the EU, with harmonized reporting.

Adopted · national schemes phasing in
2026European Union

Unsold textiles can no longer be destroyed

Under the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation, large companies may no longer destroy unsold apparel and footwear.

In force since July 2026

Sustainability

Reuse first. Recycle next.Nothing unaccounted for.

Circularity only counts when it is measured. Artus follows the waste hierarchy and records the route every lot takes.

Waste hierarchy · most preferred first

  1. 01
    Reuse & resaleWearable items are graded for second-hand, wholesale and recommerce markets first.
  2. 02
    Repair & remakeItems that can be restored go to repair and remake partners.
  3. 03
    RecyclingNon-wearable textiles become verified feedstock for recyclers and processors.
  4. 04
    RecoveryThe last route, never the default — and recorded like every other.
